Central Platform Architecture and
Interoperability Framework
Federated government revenue collection and national oversight
Version 1.0 | Proposed design | 8 October 2026

Purpose and recommended model
We propose a federated model in which each concessionaire operates its own revenue collection system within its
authorised concession and reports standardised information to a Central Revenue Oversight and Integration Platform,
referred to in this framework as the Central Platform. Government receives a consolidated view of assessments,
collections, settlements, messaging and operating performance.
This framework defines the responsibilities, architecture, data contracts, security controls and operating rules needed
to connect the independent systems. It provides a basis for consortium agreement, Government review and
subsequent detailed design. It does not award concessions, establish regulatory authority or determine software
ownership.

Design commitments
•    Concessionaire systems retain operational control of registration, assessment, billing, collection channels and
     customer support.
•    The Central Platform receives durable events and provides national monitoring, reconciliation, audit and
     approved shared services.
•    Government controls the oversight mandate, access policy, concession register and approved reporting
     standards.
•    A central outage does not normally stop authorised local collection. Queued reports are delivered and reconciled
     after recovery.
•    Central acceptance of a report is distinct from provider confirmation of payment and bank confirmation of
     settlement.

Assumptions requiring agreement
The number of concessionaires, territories, revenue types, settlement accounts and contracting entities remain to be
confirmed. iDARMS is treated as one potential concessionaire platform. Its existing assets and the assets of other
members remain subject to their verified ownership and agreed licences. All numerical service targets in this
framework are proposed planning values, not existing commitments.

Reading guide
Sections 1 to 3 cover responsibilities and architecture; sections 4 to 7 define interoperability and financial controls;
sections 8 to 10 cover security, operations and governance; sections 11 and 12 set out implementation and approval
requirements.

1 Operating model and responsibility boundaries
A concession is an authorisation to collect specified revenue within a defined scope and period. A concessionaire may
be a company or a consortium. A consortium partner does not gain collection authority solely through membership.

Authority and isolation
The Government-approved concession register defines concessionaire ID, authority, jurisdiction codes, revenue
codes, effective dates, settlement arrangements and status. Versioned changes have future or explicit effective dates.
Offline collection uses an approved cached scope with a defined expiry and risk policy; central validation flags out-of-
scope reports for investigation rather than silently discarding financial evidence.
Each concessionaire accesses its own records and approved shared references. Government and auditors access
wider data only under assigned roles. Competitors do not receive each other’s identifiable customers, pricing or
detailed operating data through shared dashboards.

Money movement
Funds move through approved payment providers and bank accounts under the concession and Government
settlement arrangements. The Central Platform records and reconciles those flows. Authority to initiate refunds,
move funds, deduct fees or distribute commissions requires a separately approved mandate and financial controls.

3 Central services and deployment design
Integration and event processing
The external reporting gateway authenticates each concessionaire, checks authorised scope and validates contract
versions. It stores accepted submissions durably before returning a receipt. An internal event bus delivers them to
transaction processing, reconciliation, messaging, audit and analytical consumers. Separate queues and quotas
prevent one concessionaire’s backlog from exhausting national capacity.
Invalid business records enter a restricted quarantine with a reason and correction route. Unauthenticated requests
are rejected. Processing failures use retries and a dead-letter queue. Accepted raw events remain available for replay,
and consumer checkpoints support rebuilding derived views without changing financial history.

Data services
•    Operational repository stores validated transaction histories, current views, concession references and exception
     cases.
•    Event archive preserves original accepted payloads, hashes, processing decisions and correction links under
     controlled retention.
•    Warehouse receives curated, minimised data for national analytics. Dashboards show reporting lag, missing feeds
     and financial verification status.
•    Audit service records administrative changes, queries of sensitive records, approvals, exports and privileged
     activity.

Deployment topology
Use separate development, test, certification and production environments. Production should span independent
failure domains with redundant gateways, processing nodes and database replicas. Place public ingress behind
network filtering and application protection; keep brokers and databases on private networks. Deploy a separately
recoverable disaster recovery environment and test failover with each concessionaire.
A practical starting stack is containerised services, a relational operational database, a durable message broker, object
storage for the event archive, and a warehouse or reporting database. Product choice follows workload tests and
support capacity. Kubernetes, Kafka, RabbitMQ and a separate data lake are options, not mandatory prerequisites. A
smaller well-operated platform can meet initial needs.

External interfaces
Independent provider and bank adapters import authenticated APIs, webhooks or protected signed files. Each feed
records origin, extraction time, coverage period and completeness. Government, identity and GIS integration uses
authorised interfaces. No connection to a national identity system is assumed until the responsible authority
approves access.

4 Interoperability rules and API catalogue
All concessionaires implement the same externally published contracts while retaining their internal technology. The
Central Platform operator maintains a sandbox, OpenAPI descriptions, event schemas, code lists, examples and a
certification suite. The API catalogue below is a proposed baseline, not an implemented interface.

Interface                                              Purpose and required behaviour

POST /v1/events                                        Submit one event; return durable ingestion ID and status URL.

POST /v1/event-batches                                 Submit bounded batches; return a result for every item.

GET /v1/ingestions/{id}                                Check received, validated, applied or quarantined status.

GET /v1/reference-data                                 Retrieve versioned authorities, revenue codes and scope data.

POST /v1/messages                                      Request an approved message with an idempotency key.

GET /v1/messages/{id}                                  Retrieve provider acceptance and delivery status separately.

GET /v1/receipts/{id}/verification                     Verify a receipt with minimal disclosure and rate limits.

POST /v1/control-totals                                Submit period counts, amounts and manifest references.

GET /v1/exceptions                                     View scoped cases; controlled actions use separate permissions.

POST /v1/provider-evidence                             Restricted independent financial-feed submission.



Transport and responses
Use HTTPS with JSON encoded in UTF-8, UTC timestamps with explicit offsets, fixed decimal money or integer minor
units, currency codes and published identifiers. Define size limits, pagination and timeouts in the contract. HTTP 202
indicates durable receipt only. Item status determines business acceptance. Invalid schemas return an explanatory
4xx response; rate limits return 429 with retry guidance. Transient 5xx errors and timeouts are retried using the
original event ID.

Authentication and versioning
Use separate machine credentials for each concessionaire and environment, short-lived OAuth access tokens, and
mutual TLS for reporting clients. Tokens identify the client and permitted scopes. Human access uses federated
OpenID Connect with multi-factor authentication. Apply OAuth security guidance [3].
Pin an OpenAPI version compatible with all partners [1], and use CloudEvents 1.0 metadata for the event envelope
[2]. API major versions and payload schema versions are explicit. Additive optional fields may be compatible; changed
meanings or mandatory fields require a new version. Proposed policy: publish breaking changes at least 180 days
before retirement, support old and new versions in parallel, and certify every partner before cutover.

The event envelope uses CloudEvents attributes id, source, specversion and type, with time, subject and dataschema
supplied by this framework. The combination of source and id identifies an event. The payload carries concession and
financial references. The gateway derives the authorised tenant from credentials and checks it against the payload.

Minimum event families
AssessmentCreated, AssessmentAmended, AssessmentCancelled; InvoiceIssued and InvoiceCancelled;
PaymentInitiated, PaymentConfirmed, PaymentFailed and PaymentReversed; ReceiptIssued; RefundRequested,
RefundApproved and RefundCompleted; SettlementReported and SettlementConfirmed; AgentStatusChanged and
DeviceStatusChanged; MessageRequested and MessageDeliveryUpdated. Independent provider evidence remains
distinguishable from concessionaire reports.

Identity and reference policy
Account, person, property, business and invoice are separate entities. A phone number is a contact channel and must
not be treated as a unique person identifier. Use approved identity references only where needed. Concessionaire
account IDs map to a central opaque reference through a controlled process; uncertain matches require review.
Ownership changes to a property or business preserve historical liability and payment records.
Money allocations support partial payments, one payment against several invoices, and several payments against one
invoice. A failed attempt creates no collected revenue. Refunds, reversals and amendments preserve original records
and link compensating entries. Published code lists define each state and its allowed transitions.

6 Reliable reporting and offline recovery
Local commit and durable delivery
The concessionaire writes the business transaction and its reporting event to a durable outbox in the same local
database transaction, or implements an equivalent tested atomic mechanism. A delivery worker sends pending
events, retains receipt IDs and retries with exponential backoff and jitter. A lost acknowledgement triggers resend of
the same event, not creation of a new financial event.
The central gateway durably records the envelope before acknowledgement. Consumers apply an event
idempotently, so repeated delivery produces one business effect. A duplicate with the same identity and matching
payload returns the original status. The same identity with different content is quarantined as a conflict. Delivery is at
least once; the design does not assume exactly-once network transport.

Ordering and completeness
Ordering applies per business object, using an aggregate version. A refund arriving before its payment waits for its
predecessor or enters a dependency exception. There is no requirement for a single global sequence. Late events
retain their occurred time and central received time so accounting-period updates remain traceable.
Each partner sends period manifests containing event counts, financial totals by currency and event family, first and
last sequence markers, and missing sequence declarations where applicable. Compare these with central receipts and
applied events. A heartbeat confirms connectivity only; it does not prove that all transactions were reported.

Offline collection
Field devices use encrypted local storage, unique local references, authenticated operators and approved cached
concession rules. Offline payment capture must distinguish a collection claim from confirmed electronic payment.
Receipts indicate provisional status where confirmation is unavailable. Cash collection requires separate deposit and
reconciliation controls. Device synchronisation feeds the concessionaire system before central reporting.

Central outage and backlog recovery
•    Keep collecting within the approved continuity policy and preserve the local outbox.
•    Show Government that the national view is stale, with last received time by concessionaire.
•    On recovery, throttle replay, prioritise recent critical events without losing backlog, and preserve business
     ordering.
•    Compare control totals and independent evidence; resolve missing and conflicting records through exception
     cases.
•    Remove local queue entries only under the agreed retention and acceptance policy; retain replay evidence.
Reporting failures do not automatically reverse a payment. Any decision to restrict collection follows authorised
operational procedures rather than a gateway timeout.

7 Reconciliation messaging and national reporting
Independent financial reconciliation
Match concessionaire reports against provider confirmation, provider settlement statements and destination bank or
treasury records. A reported payment, a confirmed provider payment, a settled amount and a reconciled Government
credit are separate states. National reports expose all four. Central reconciliation is authoritative only to the extent
that evidence coverage and unresolved exceptions support it.
Match using provider references, amount, currency, time window and allocation IDs. Settlement batches may
combine many payments; store a batch-to-payment allocation. Compare gross collections, approved fees, refunds,
chargebacks, timing differences and net credited funds. An unmatched difference is an exception until classified, not
automatically fraud.

Financial approvals and exceptions
Assign each exception an owner, severity, evidence links, due date and status. Refund approval, bank-detail changes
and reconciliation overrides use maker-checker approval and reason codes. Freeze original evidence. Corrections
generate linked events, and closed accounting periods require controlled adjustment entries. Rules distinguish
pending settlements from overdue ones using approved provider calendars.

Shared messaging
Concessionaires submit a business notification request with recipient reference, template version, language, channel,
transaction reference and deduplication key. The central service applies approved sender identity, routing, consent or
other authorised communication basis, rate limits and cost allocation. It records request, provider acceptance,
delivery outcome and failure separately.
An uncertain delivery result must not trigger uncontrolled cross-channel duplicates. Fallback policy defines timing,
alternate channels and whether a message can be resent. Transactional and promotional communication have
different controls. A local fallback gateway, if approved, reports its outcomes centrally and uses the same notification
identity. USSD is normally a customer-initiated session; session integration is distinct from SMS or push delivery.

National dashboards and measures
Display assessments, confirmed collections, outstanding balances, settled funds, unresolved differences and
collection efficiency by authority, period, concession and revenue type. Define numerator, denominator, exclusions
and period for every KPI. Separate payment attempts from successful collections. Mark late or incomplete data;
present receipt time and last completed reconciliation alongside totals.
Auditors can trace a national figure through warehouse records to applied events and independent evidence. Receipt
verification discloses only the fields necessary to establish authenticity. Automated anomaly rules raise review cases;
they do not independently impose sanctions or authorise financial changes.

8 Security privacy and data governance
Access and trust boundaries
Government oversight, concessionaire operations, external financial feeds and platform administration are distinct
trust zones. Enforce tenant and jurisdiction scope at the API, application and data layers. Concessionaire identifiers
supplied by clients never override authenticated scope. Human roles separate national viewing, audit, operations,
finance approval and technical administration.
•    Use multi-factor authentication for privileged users, time-limited elevated access and documented emergency
     access.
•    Rotate client certificates and credentials, revoke compromised clients and keep secrets in managed storage.
•    Encrypt network traffic, databases, archives and backups; document key ownership and recovery procedures.
•    Protect ingress with quotas, request validation and application filtering. Test authorisation across tenants.
•    Record sensitive exports, data changes and administrative actions with actor, time, scope and reason.

Data minimisation and lawful access
Approve a field-level sharing schedule before integration. The central oversight dataset should contain only the
identity and contact data needed for authorised reporting, reconciliation and messaging. Keep contact details and
identity mappings in restricted services. Use synthetic or irreversibly de-identified records for testing. Exclude card
security codes, full payment credentials and unnecessary identity-document images from event payloads.
Confirm data-controller and processor responsibilities, hosting locations, permitted transfers, retention periods,
incident duties and access arrangements through the appropriate Government and legal review. This framework
proposes technical controls; it does not determine the lawful basis or declare any particular hosting location legally
sufficient.

Records and citizen rights
Data governance must distinguish Government records, personal data, concessionaire business information and
software IP. Access to records follows the Government mandate and applicable agreements; holding data does not
itself establish proprietary ownership. Provide approved correction, access and deletion processes with documented
exceptions for required financial retention.
An append-only archive protects audit integrity during an approved retention period. It is not a promise to retain all
personal data indefinitely. Partition or tokenise personal fields so retention expiry and authorised deletion can be
implemented without destroying the minimum financial evidence lawfully retained.

Incident handling
Maintain a common incident procedure with named contacts, detection and containment steps, evidence
preservation, credential revocation and required notification routes. The central operator and affected concessionaire
coordinate recovery, while Government retains oversight. Test data exposure, fraudulent client submissions and
compromised field-device scenarios before production.

Sizing and data integrity
Obtain transactions per day, peak events per second, events per payment, payload sizes, messaging volume, offline
backlog, provider schedules and retention periods. Size for measured peaks and concurrent catch-up. Estimate
archive growth from event count × mean payload size × retention, then add indexes, replication and backups. Load
tests include partner isolation and reconciliation workload.
A nonzero disaster-recovery RPO can lose recently acknowledged events. Partners must retain accepted outbox
history for an agreed replay window and compare manifests after failover. An ingestion status that disappears after
recovery triggers safe replay. If durable acceptance must survive without loss, specify and test an RPO of zero with an
appropriate replication design and cost.

Monitoring and operational evidence
Monitor backlog depth, oldest pending event, rejection rate, duplicate conflicts, delayed predecessors, consumer lag,
reporting completeness, provider-feed freshness and warehouse lag. Alerts have an owner and response playbook.
Monitor certificate expiry, failed authentication, unusual exports and backup success alongside service performance.

Recovery verification
Test backup restoration, primary-region loss, database failure, broker recovery and corrupted projections. Rebuild
derived views from retained events and verify financial totals. Recovery is complete only when service health, event
completeness and financial reconciliation meet the agreed criteria. Messaging requires its own replay rules to prevent
resending old notifications.

10 Governance ownership and concession transition
Government and shared operator
Government should approve the oversight standards, concession scope, reporting access, KPI definitions and audit
policy. A designated Central Platform operator implements and supports those controls under an approved service
agreement. The operator may be Government, an independent provider or an SPV; selection remains open.
If concessionaires jointly own the operator, establish Government oversight, independent audit, conflict disclosure,
access separation and a route for admitting future concessionaires on equivalent terms. Equity participation does not
grant unrestricted access to competitors’ data or unilateral control over interoperability rules.

Intellectual property and funding
Maintain a Background IP register for each participant, including iDARMS where applicable, identifying owners,
components, licences and third-party restrictions. Interface conformity does not require disclosure of local source
code. Separate ownership of central code, central operating licences, Government usage rights, local applications and
the public interoperability specification.
Agree central development and operating costs separately from collection revenue shares. Allocate costs
transparently using fixed participation, measured usage or another approved basis. Prevent central-service fees from
being deducted from public collections without express authorisation. Government continuity rights, source access or
escrow arrangements require explicit terms.

Concession expiry or replacement
Use effective-dated authority changes and identify who services outstanding assessments, refunds and disputes.
Freeze new collection rights at the agreed cutover while permitting authorised historical reporting. Export agreed
data and evidence to Government or an approved successor, rotate credentials and reconcile balances. Transfer of
records does not automatically transfer proprietary software. Test transition through a rehearsal before expiry.

Mandatory integration tests
•    Duplicate submission and lost acknowledgement cause one business effect.
•    Different payloads using the same event identity are flagged without overwriting evidence.
•    Out-of-order payment, receipt, reversal and refund events resolve consistently.
•    A batch with mixed valid and invalid items returns individual results without silent losses.
•    A concessionaire cannot read or submit records under another concessionaire’s identity.
•    Offline replay and central failover restore completeness against control totals.
•    Partial payments, multi-invoice allocations, fees and settlement batches reconcile correctly.
•    Missing bank evidence, provider delay and unexplained differences appear as open exceptions.
•    Message retry, fallback and callback replay do not create unauthorised duplicate notifications.
•    Credential revocation, expired concessions and approved historical reporting behave as specified.

Production readiness
Before launch, approve runbooks, support contacts, monitoring, recovery evidence, penetration-test findings, privacy
controls and Government access. Record defects, owners and acceptance decisions. A partner is certified for a
specific schema and scope; material changes require targeted recertification. Dates and rollout volumes should be set
after discovery rather than inferred from this framework.

12 Decisions and detailed design deliverables
Decisions for the next joint meeting
•     Confirm the collecting entities, concession boundaries and responsible Government authority.
•     Approve the separation of local operations, central oversight and independent financial evidence.
•     Select the central operating and ownership model, including conflict safeguards.
•     Agree mandatory reporting fields, event families and financial state definitions.
•     Confirm whether messaging is mandatory centrally or permits audited local fallback.
•     Approve settlement mandates, evidence access, fee treatment and exception ownership.
•     Set hosting, data access, retention and identity integration policies.
•     Assign funding, delivery responsibilities, pilot participants and acceptance approvers.

Deliverables after approval
The detailed design should produce a system context and deployment diagram; OpenAPI files; JSON event schemas;
event and code-list registries; example requests and responses; data-sharing and retention schedules; concession
scope rules; authentication profiles; reconciliation rules; financial allocation models; messaging templates and
fallback rules; sizing and service-level schedules; recovery runbooks; partner certification scripts; and a responsibility
matrix with named owners.
